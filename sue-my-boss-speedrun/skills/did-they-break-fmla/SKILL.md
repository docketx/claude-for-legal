# /did-they-break-fmla

Read `../../CLAUDE.md` and `../../../employment-law/CLAUDE.md` first. Answer only from
`../../../employment-law/references/` (one `rows-<jurisdiction>-employment.json` per jurisdiction: `us`, `ca`, `tx`, `wa`, `nj`), clause `family-medical-leave`.

## Ask these three before anything else

They change which row applies, and people rarely volunteer them.

1. **Is the employer a state or local government body?** If it is a state agency, the answer splits:
   the pack's row carries *Hibbs* for family-care leave and *Coleman* for self-care leave, and they
   come out opposite ways on suing for money. If it is a private employer, that split is irrelevant
   and saying otherwise will mislead them badly.
2. **Was the leave for someone else, or for yourself?** Same reason.
3. **How much leave have you already had this year, and was any of it for the same reason?** The
   entitlement is a ceiling. An employer that already gave twelve qualifying weeks has met the Act.

## What the pack establishes

Render the matching rows with their quotes and sources. The three points that carry most of the work:

- **Twelve workweeks in a twelve-month period**, described by the Supreme Court as the Act's most
  fundamental substantive guarantee. It is unpaid. It is also a ceiling: in *Ragsdale* the employee
  had received thirty weeks and still lost.
- **A notice failure is not, by itself, the violation.** A regulation once said undesignated leave
  did not count against the entitlement, and the Court struck it down. After *Ragsdale* the employee
  has to show the failure actually cost them something — that they would have taken the leave
  differently, or at another time. Bring the prejudice, not just the missing form.
- **Against a state employer**, family-care leave can be sued on for damages and self-care leave
  cannot. Two people at the same agency can have different remedies. Neither case says anything about
  a private employer.

## What you must say is not established

The pack's `notes` field lists this and it is long. At minimum, whenever it bears on the question:

- whether the person is an **eligible employee** — the service, hours and employer-size thresholds are
  not in our corpus;
- which **conditions and relationships qualify**;
- **certification** requirements and what an employer may demand;
- **intermittent leave** in any form;
- what the **current regulations** say, because we do not hold the Code of Federal Regulations —
  *Ragsdale* struck down one version of one regulation and the pack does not establish what replaced it.

Say these plainly. A reader who thinks the pack has cleared their eligibility will act on an answer
that was never given.

## Closing

Do not say whether the Act was broken. Give the rules, the traps, the gaps, and one line: this is a
draft for attorney review, not legal advice. If leave has already been denied and anything has been
announced to them, add the step-2 line from `sue-my-boss-speedrun`: a clock may already be running,
speak to a lawyer today.
