quote from a corpus we publish, re-checked against it, or is recorded as not established**.

authority; 3 record an honest absence, which this pack treats as a finding rather than a gap to fill.

## The limit you need to know before you read anything else

**We publish the United States Code, and for most of this pack's life we did not know it.** `docketx/us-statutes`
carries 60,433 sections at release point Public Law 119-103, and the FMLA and Title VII sections are quoted here
directly from it. We do NOT publish the Code of Federal Regulations, so anything resting on an agency regulation
is still described rather than quoted.

Where a court's words are the authority — a holding, not statutory text — the pack quotes the case and says so.
Where the statute is the authority, the pack quotes the statute. The FMLA entitlement and its eligibility test
come from § 2612(a)(1) and § 2611(2)(A); the Title VII charge period comes from § 2000e–5(e)(1).

## Deadlines

The charge-filing row is built on *Delaware State College v. Ricks* and *Mohasco Corp. v. Silver*, both 1980.
Limitation periods, deferral-state lists and accrual rules have moved since. **Treat every date in this pack
as a reason to speak to a lawyer today, not as time you can bank.** The pack will not compute a date for you,
and no skill in it will tell you that you still have time.

## What each rule carries

- `question` — what a person actually asks, in their words.
- `rule` — the answer, with the operative text quoted inside it.
- `trap` — the thing that makes a normal-looking situation come out differently than expected.
- `authority[]` — each with a verbatim quote, the dataset and row it came from, and the date checked.
- `notes` — **what verification could not establish.** This is the most important field in the file. For the
  FMLA rows it covers eligibility thresholds, which conditions qualify, certification, and intermittent leave.

## The traps, in one place

- **Twelve weeks is a ceiling as well as a floor.** Ragsdale received thirty weeks of leave and still lost.
- **A notice failure is not itself the violation.** The employee must show it actually cost them something.
- **A state employer can be sued for family-care leave and not for self-care leave** — *Hibbs* and *Coleman*
  come out opposite ways. Neither says anything about a private employer.
- **The charge clock starts when you are told**, not when the consequences arrive.
- **A Washington non-compete can fail on timing alone**, however reasonable its terms.
- **CEPA usually expects the employer to have had a chance to fix it first**, subject to the section's own
  exceptions.

## What this pack will not do

It will not tell you whether you have a claim. It will not name or characterise a real employer or manager.
It will not draft something as if it were ready to file. It will not answer for a state it does not cover.

Every output is a draft for attorney review, not legal advice.
