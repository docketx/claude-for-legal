# employment-law — how to answer from this pack

Read this before running any skill in this plugin.

## The one rule that matters

**Answer from `references/` (one `rows-<jurisdiction>-employment.json` per jurisdiction: `us`, `ca`, `tx`, `wa`, `nj`), never from memory.** If the pack has no row for the
jurisdiction and topic asked about, say so and stop. A confident answer assembled from general knowledge is
the failure this pack exists to prevent, and in employment law it is expensive: the reader is usually a person
deciding whether to sign something, file something, or let a deadline pass.

## The jurisdiction gate

| jurisdiction | what this pack holds |
|---|---|
| `us` (federal) | FMLA entitlement, FMLA notice failures, FMLA against a state employer, Title VII charge deadline |
| `ca` | final pay and the waiting-time penalty, non-competition |
| `tx` | final pay, the 180-day state discrimination deadline, workers'-compensation retaliation |
| `wa` | non-competition |
| `nj` | retaliation and whistleblowing (CEPA) |

Everything else is **not covered**. Not "probably similar", not "generally the law is". A question about New
York, Florida or anywhere else gets: this pack does not cover that state.

**Two deadlines can apply at once.** Texas has its own 180-day state period and the federal charge period is
separate, with a different starting point. Never let an answer imply that satisfying one satisfies the other.

## The federal statutes are not in our corpus, and you must say so

We publish state codes, the United States Code (60,433 sections) and Supreme Court opinions. We do **not**
publish the Code of Federal Regulations. So every federal statutory phrase in this pack is quoted **through** an opinion that
sets it out, and the citation names both the opinion and the section it is quoting.

Both provenances are now honest, and they are different claims. "29 U.S.C. § 2612(a)(1) provides …" is a
statement about the statute, which we hold. "The Supreme Court in Ragsdale held …" is a statement about a
holding, which no statute can supply. Do not attribute a holding to the Code, or the Code to a court.

## Deadlines get a louder warning than anything else

The charge-filing row is built on opinions from 1980. Limitation periods, deferral-state lists and the rules
for how a claim accrues have all moved since, and a deadline is the one thing where being out of date is the
same as being wrong. Whenever a deadline appears in an answer:

1. Give the rule and its source.
2. Say plainly that it comes from a 1980 opinion and is not a current-date calculator.
3. Tell the reader to treat it as a reason to speak to a lawyer **today**, not as time they can bank.

Never compute a specific date for someone. Never tell someone they still have time.

## Source tags

Every legal statement carries one:

- **VERIFIED** — a verbatim quote from our corpus, with the case or section named. The quote is the authority.
- **NOT ESTABLISHED** — the pack looked and could not verify it. Say what is missing, not what you assume.
- **NOT COVERED** — outside the jurisdiction gate above.

A row with `"status": "no-verified-authority"` is a *finding*, not an absence. Render it. The New Jersey
family-leave row says the source is in our corpus and the work is not done; that is materially different from
"no such law", and a reader who is told "nothing here" will draw the wrong conclusion.

## What this pack never does

- Never characterises a specific real employer, manager or colleague.
- Never tells someone they have a claim, or that they do not.
- Never drafts a demand, charge or complaint as if it were ready to file.
- Never fills a gap in the pack with general knowledge, however confident.

Every output is a draft for attorney review. Say so.
